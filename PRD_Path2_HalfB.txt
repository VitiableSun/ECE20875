3. QUESTION 3: DAY-OF-WEEK PATTERNS AND CLASSIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This question has two parts. Both must be completed.

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
PART A -- Visualization: Weekly Traffic Patterns
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Goal:
  Analyze whether bike traffic shows consistent patterns across days of the
  week. Visualize those patterns.

Method:
  - Group the dataset by the Day column.
  - Compute the mean Total bike count for each day of the week.
  - Plot the result as a bar chart or line plot with days in order
    (Monday through Sunday on the x-axis, average Total on the y-axis).
  - Optionally: also plot per-bridge averages by day (grouped bar chart)
    for richer insight.

Ordering note:
  The Day column contains strings. When grouping and plotting, sort days in
  the correct weekly order (Mon, Tue, Wed, Thu, Fri, Sat, Sun) rather than
  alphabetical order.

Expected deliverable from code:
  - A bar chart or line plot of average Total by day of week.
  - Print the average Total per day to the console as well.

Interpretation to address:
  - Are weekdays consistently higher or lower than weekends?
  - Is there a clear peak day or trough day?
  - Does the pattern make intuitive sense (e.g., commuters driving up
    weekday counts)?

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
PART B -- Classification: Predict Day of Week
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Goal:
  Build a classifier that predicts the day of the week (7 classes: Monday
  through Sunday) from the four bridge counts alone.

Features (X): Brooklyn Bridge, Manhattan Bridge, Williamsburg Bridge,
              Queensboro Bridge
Target (y):   Day of week (encoded as integer 0-6)

Train/test split:
  - Use an 80/20 train/test split (random_state=42 for reproducibility).
  - Fit the classifier on training data, evaluate on test data.
  - Note: with ~214 rows total, the test set will be ~43 rows. This is small,
    so results may vary; acknowledge this in the report.

Classifier choice:
  - Pick one (or more) classifier(s). Good options:
      * LogisticRegression (simple, interpretable baseline)
      * KNeighborsClassifier (non-parametric, good for small datasets)
      * DecisionTreeClassifier (interpretable, handles non-linearity)
      * RandomForestClassifier (more robust, ensemble)
  - You must justify your choice in the report.
  - If you test multiple classifiers, compare their accuracy.

Evaluation:
  - Report overall accuracy on the test set.
  - Produce a confusion matrix to see which days are confused with each other.
  - Optional: print a classification report (precision, recall, F1 per class).

Expected deliverable from code:
  - Print test accuracy for the chosen classifier.
  - Display or print the confusion matrix.
  - Optional: bar chart of per-class accuracy or the full classification report.

Report content -- what to discuss:
  - Is accuracy high or low? (Given only 4 features and 7 classes, do not
    necessarily expect high accuracy.)
  - Which days are easiest to classify correctly (likely weekends, which have
    distinct lower traffic patterns)?
  - Which days are hardest (likely mid-week days that look similar to each
    other)?
  - Does the confusion matrix reveal that the model confuses adjacent weekdays
    more than weekdays vs. weekends?
  - Why might classification be inherently difficult here? (Only 4 numeric
    features, 7 classes, small dataset, overlapping distributions between
    similar weekdays.)
